Calibration in openJII is done at a bench: the device is connected to your
computer, the browser runs a calibration procedure against it and any reference
instruments, a script fits coefficients from the readings, and you decide whether
to apply them. Completed captures are kept on the device's record, so you can see
which coefficients are in force and where they came from. Stopping a capture before
it finishes leaves no run on record.

<Callout type="info">
  Calibration is currently in internal testing and available only to accounts with calibration
  enabled. If **Calibrations** does not appear under **Devices** in the sidebar, it is not available
  to you yet.
</Callout>

## What you need

- A device registered in **Devices** that you can contribute to. Recording a
  bench run requires contribute access; approving, rejecting or writing it needs
  **Can edit** on the device, or Owner or Admin of the organization that owns it.
  Only people who can manage a device see its **Calibration** tab.
- A Chromium-based browser on a desktop. The browser is the bridge to the
  hardware over Web Serial; without Web Serial, **Run at the bench** stays
  disabled.
- The reference instruments the procedure names, each on a serial port of its
  own.

## Calibration procedures

**Calibrations** in the sidebar lists the procedures you can see, with their
device family, visibility and when they were last updated. A procedure says
which device family it is for, which instruments the bench needs, the steps it
walks, the coefficients it produces and the script that fits them. Its page
shows these as **The rig it needs**, **What it does at the bench**, **What it
produces** and **How it fits**, with the device family and minimum firmware in
the details beside them. A run on firmware older than that minimum is refused.

**New calibration** creates a procedure straight away, named "Untitled
calibration" and set up for a MiniPAR, and opens it so you can edit it. Once a
run has recorded a procedure, it can no longer be edited; use **Duplicate** to
start a new one from it.

<Callout type="warn">
  A calibration procedure is **public** from the moment it is created, including one made with **New
  calibration** or **Duplicate**, and public cannot be undone. Everyone who has calibration switched
  on can find and read it. Do not put anything in a procedure that you would not share.
</Callout>

## Calibrate a device

Start from the device's **Calibration** tab with **Calibrate**, or from a
procedure's page with **Run at the bench**. The wizard has six steps.

1. **Procedure.** Pick the procedure for this device.
2. **Connect.** Select **Connect device** and choose the device's port, then
   connect each instrument the procedure needs. The run cannot start until the
   device answers as the family the procedure is for and every required
   instrument is connected. Started from a device, the wizard also checks the
   serial number the unit reports against that device's record, and a different
   serial stops the run; a unit that reports no serial cannot be checked, so make
   sure the right one is on the port. Started from a procedure's page, the
   wizard matches the unit to one of your registered devices by its serial, or
   asks **Which device is this?** when the firmware does not report one.
3. **Capture.** **Start the run** walks the procedure and records each reading.
   Answer what it asks along the way. **Stop** ends the run without recording
   anything.
4. **Review.** The result lists each block and the coefficients of any block that
   computed. If the script supplies a quality verdict, the card shows **Quality
   gates passed** or **Quality gates failed**. R² and NRMSE appear when the
   script supplies those numbers with the verdict. A chart appears when the
   result includes usable fitted-point data or vector coefficients. **Approve**
   applies the computed coefficients to the device's record, replacing any
   earlier ones for the same block.
   **Reject** applies nothing and keeps the run on record. Only a run that
   produced coefficients can be approved. Approving does not touch the hardware.
5. **Write.** **Write to device** writes each approved block and confirms it by
   reading it back. A block that is not confirmed may have reached the device in
   part, so run the calibration again before trusting the device. **Finish
   without writing** leaves the coefficients approved on record only.
6. **Done.** A summary of what was measured, approved and confirmed on the
   device.

Only MiniPAR and Ambit coefficients can be written to the device. For every
other family, including MultispeQ, the write step says the calibration is
approved on record only.

## What is in force

A device's **Calibration** tab shows the **In force** card and the **Bench
sessions** list. **In force** shows the coefficients the device's record holds,
since when, and whether they were **Written to device**, are **Not yet written to
device**, or were **Written but not confirmed**. The device's overview has a
**Calibration** card with the date it was last calibrated and a link to this tab.

Each bench session keeps its status (Running, Computed, Compute failed, Error,
Approved or Rejected), what the bench measured, the review and any write.
Opening a session that is still waiting for a decision gives you **Approve** and
**Reject**. A session that was approved but never written offers **Write to
device**, so you can write it later from the bench.
