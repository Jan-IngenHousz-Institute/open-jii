A continuous sensor records observations over time without a person starting
each reading, unlike a handheld MultispeQ. Bringing that data into openJII is a
**generic MQTT capability**: any autonomous device can publish measurements to
the platform's IoT endpoint once it is registered and holds credentials.

**Ambyte** is JII's own edge device built on exactly this capability: a field
gateway that receives readings from autonomous sensors and measurement devices,
such as the **Ambit**, and publishes their observations to the platform over
MQTT. Ambit (`ambit`) and **MiniPAR** (`minipar`) are separate measurement-device
families; MiniPAR is normally operated locally.

## Register the device and issue credentials

Registration and credentials are the same for any continuous sensor:

1. Open **Devices** and register the device using its physical identifier.
2. Issue device credentials (certificates) from the registered device's
   **Credentials** tab.
3. Install the credentials on the device or its gateway so it can authenticate
   against the platform's MQTT endpoint.

The device's status follows these steps. A device is **Registered** once it is in
the registry, **Provisioned** once it holds a certificate, and **Onboarded** once
it is bound to at least one experiment. **Revoked** means its certificate was
withdrawn and a new one restores it. **Retired** takes a device out of service on
purpose: its certificate is revoked, it drops out of attention lists and pickers,
and its record and history stay until you reinstate or delete it. Whether the
device is currently on the broker is a separate live indicator beside the status:
Online, Offline, Never connected, or Unknown when the fleet index is unavailable.
A phone has no certificate and picks its experiment in the app, so it shows the
connectivity indicator only, reading Idle when the app is closed. Retiring one is
the exception: it cuts the phone off by detaching the identity the app signs in
with, and a retired phone carries the Retired badge like any other device.

<Callout type="warn">
  Device credentials include private key material that is shown once. Store it securely and never
  paste it into an issue, screenshot, or shared document.
</Callout>

## Where the data goes

A registered device publishes its measurements over MQTT; the platform routes
them through the ingestion pipeline into the experiment's data tables. The topic
structure, message schema, and connection details are developer material: see
[Data ingestion](/developers/architecture/data-ingestion) in the Developers tab
and the [MQTT API reference](/api/mqtt).

## See the devices behind an experiment

Every experiment has a **Devices** tab. It lists two kinds of device together:

- Devices **onboarded** to the experiment from their own Onboarding tab.
- Devices that **sent data** into the experiment in the last 30 days, whether or
  not they were onboarded. A publisher that matches no registered device is
  listed by its client id as an unregistered publisher.

Four tiles summarize the roster: onboarded, sending data, onboarded with no
recent data, and sending without onboarding.

Below them the devices are listed down the left, searchable and paged 25 at a
time, with the first one selected. Picking a device fills the rest of the page
with its detail: status and live broker connectivity, the firmware version and
battery level it last reported, how much it has sent into this experiment both in
the window and in total, when data last arrived from it anywhere, and its
onboarding date. A chart under the facts shows what it sent into this experiment
day by day. Onboarded devices can be detached from here, and a device you have
access to links through to its full monitoring.

An unregistered publisher is listed by the name it reports for itself, falling
back to its client id.

This replaces the separate device metadata table that used to sit among the
experiment's data tables: the same reported facts now appear beside each device's
registry identity and live state.

![The experiment Devices tab: four summary tiles above a table of onboarded gateways, one gateway sending without onboarding, and an unregistered publisher listed by client id](/img/guide/web/experiment-devices.webp)

Anyone who can view or edit the experiment can open an onboarded device from this
tab, even without their own access to the device. A device that only sent data
still appears with its facts, but opens only if you already have access to it;
a lock marks those rows. The tab is not shown to people who reach a public
experiment through public visibility alone.

<Callout type="info">
  The measurement counts and last-arrival times come from the data pipeline and lag behind the live
  broker by the pipeline's cadence. If the pipeline cannot be reached, the tab says so and does not
  count any device as silent.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
  `ambyte` and `ambit` are distinct stored values today, but their history is not clean: database
  migration `0037` remapped existing `ambit` rows to `ambyte`, and migration `0038` later re-added
  `ambit`. A legacy `ambyte` row may therefore hold data captured by an Ambit. openJII records this
  ambiguity and follows the current stored value; it does not rewrite historical rows.
</Callout>

## Bring in Ambyte data

- Register the Ambyte from **Devices** and issue its credentials as above.
- Upload an Ambyte folder to an experiment data table. The uploader expects an
  `Ambyte_N/[1-4]/*.txt` folder layout and processes the data asynchronously.
  Once the upload starts you can close the dialog and keep working, but keep the
  browser tab open until the upload finishes.
- You get a notification when the table is ready to query.

## Connect Ambit or MiniPAR locally

The web workbook host identifies Ambit and MiniPAR devices over USB serial and
selects the matching driver automatically. The Android app does the same for a
device connected by USB cable. Both families run protocol cells over a direct
connection and return a measurement envelope; see
[Writing protocols](/guide/devices-protocols/writing-protocols#target-the-right-device-family)
for the protocol shape. Command cells cover single operations such as
calibration commands and spot readings.

If the family selected in the workbook toolbar differs from what the device
reports, the web host shows a **Different device identified** notice and uses the
identified family.

The web host connects local devices through the browser: **USB/serial** over Web
Serial, and **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)** over Web Bluetooth. Browsers cannot
reach Bluetooth Classic (BR/EDR), so a Bluetooth-Classic device such as a
MultispeQ connects over USB/serial on the web rather than over the browser's
Bluetooth.

### Connect a MiniPAR to the Android app

1. Connect the MiniPAR to the phone with a USB cable or adapter. Use a powered
   USB hub when connecting several devices.
2. Open **Devices** from the device chip and select **Connect** beside the wired
   device.
3. Approve Android's USB permission request when it appears.

Before the app has identified the device, the Devices sheet names it by its USB
vendor and product numbers and the port's number, in the form
`vendor:product #number`. Once it is identified, the connected row shows the
name the MiniPAR reports, with **Measurement device** underneath.

## Ambyte support by surface

Ambyte behaves differently in three separate contexts; keep them distinct:

- **Web protocol testing** routes the `ambyte` family through the generic device
  driver over USB/serial only. This is a compatibility path for exercising a
  protocol, not a dedicated Ambyte integration.
- **Mobile local connection and execution** has no Ambyte path. The mobile app
  cannot connect to an Ambyte for measurement.
- A **product-specific Ambyte BLE integration** is planned but not yet built. Do
  not read the generic web route above as that dedicated support.

## Current limits

For its normal workflow, Ambyte is an MQTT and folder-ingestion family rather
than a locally connected workbook device. A registered device has a
**Monitoring** tab, showing its broker connectivity and what its data looks like
over time, and, for people who can manage it, an **Onboarding** tab for choosing
the experiments it streams into and handing it its configuration.
