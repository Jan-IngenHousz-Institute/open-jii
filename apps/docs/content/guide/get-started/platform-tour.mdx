Once you are signed in, everything in openJII lives inside one consistent shell.
This page is a quick visual tour of that shell so you know where to look before
diving into experiments and data.

## The sidebar

The left sidebar is your main navigation: experiments, protocols, macros,
workbooks, organizations, and devices. You can collapse it to get more room for your data; when
collapsed, hovering the left edge peeks it back open, and the button in the compact
page header reopens it permanently. Near the bottom, **What's New** lists recent platform
releases, the bell holds your **Notifications**, and **Documentation** opens this
site. Your profile menu, the light/dark theme toggle, and the language selector sit
in the footer.
See <PlatformLink path="/releases">openJII Releases</PlatformLink> for the full history.

![Collapsed sidebar peeking open on hover](/img/chrome-refresh/sidebar-peek.webp)

## The command palette

Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on macOS) anywhere
to open the command palette. It searches pages and actions as well as the
experiments, protocols, macros, workbooks, and organizations you can access.
Matches from every resource type share one relevance-ranked list, so the closest
match appears first. Select a result to open it without reaching for the mouse.

![Command palette open over Home](/img/chrome-refresh/command-palette.webp)

## Keyboard shortcuts

Most navigation has a keyboard shortcut. Press <kbd>G</kbd> followed by <kbd>O</kbd>
to open Organizations. Press <kbd>?</kbd> to open the shortcut cheatsheet and see
what is available on the page you are on.

![Keyboard shortcut cheatsheet](/img/chrome-refresh/cheatsheet.webp)

## Notifications

The bell near the bottom of the sidebar is where the platform tells you something
happened: somebody asked to join one of your experiments or organizations, a
request you made was approved or declined, somebody joined or left something you
administer, your role in an organization changed or you were removed from it,
somebody gave you, changed or removed your access to something shared with you
directly, something shared with you was deleted or moved to another
organization, or an experiment you administer went public because its embargo
ended. A dot on the bell means you have something unread.

Opening a notification marks it read and takes you to what it is about. A join
request opens that experiment's Collaborators page or that organization's Members
page, where you approve or decline it; an approval opens the experiment or
organization you were let into. A declined request links nowhere, since there is
nothing left to open. An invitation to an organization arrives here too, and opens
your Invitations tab, where you accept or decline it.

**Mark all read** clears the dot and nothing else — a join request you have not
decided is still pending and still in the list. The bell shows the ten most
recent; **See all notifications** opens the full history. Press <kbd>G</kbd>
followed by <kbd>N</kbd> to open the bell from anywhere.

![The notification bell open from the sidebar, with unread rows](/img/chrome-refresh/notifications-bell.webp)

The full history heads its rows with the day they arrived — Today, Yesterday and
Earlier. **All** and **Unread** switch between everything and what you have not
opened yet, and the category filter narrows the list to one kind: join requests
and invitations, membership and access, project transfers, and the rest. Twenty
rows fit on a page, and **Previous** and **Next** appear once there are more.
Read notifications are kept for 90 days and unread ones for a year; older ones
are removed.

![The notifications page with day groups, the All and Unread tabs and the category filter](/img/chrome-refresh/notifications-page.webp)

Data exports and uploads do not appear here. To check on an export, open the
dataset's export list on the experiment's **Data** page.

### Which notifications also reach you by email

The gear in the bell's header — or **Account → Notifications** — opens one email
switch per category. Switching a category off only stops the email: every
notification still arrives in the bell, whatever the switches say. Account
security is the exception you cannot turn off, and sign-in codes arrive whatever
you choose here.

Today only **Requests and invitations**, **Membership and access**,
**Experiments and resources** and **Project transfers** can be switched. The
other categories are listed but greyed out until their notifications start
arriving by email, and each becomes available on its own as those notifications
are added.

![The Notifications tab in account settings, with one email switch per category](/img/guide/web/account-security/notification-settings.webp)

## Home

Home is the page you land on after signing in, and **Home** at the top of the
sidebar brings you back to it. On a wide window it has two columns. The main
column holds what you opened recently, public research and blog posts, and the
narrower column beside it holds your unread notifications, your last 30 days,
what is new on the platform and where to report a problem. On a narrow window
or a phone the sections stack in one column, with your unread notifications
first. Apart from Notifications, a section with nothing to show is left out,
heading and all.

A notice can sit above the sections, one at a time. When the community passes
a measurement milestone, the notice marks it. Otherwise it welcomes you and
links to **Request transfer**, where you ask to bring a project over from
PhotosynQ (see [Project transfers](/guide/sharing/project-transfers)). Close a
notice with its × and it stays closed in that browser.

### Recently opened

**Recently opened** lists the five experiments, protocols, macros and workbooks
you opened most recently, newest first. Opening the overview page of any of
them counts as a visit. Each row shows the type, one detail that suits it, your
role when you hold one, and when you opened it. The detail is an experiment's
status and organization, a protocol's device family, a macro's language or a
workbook's latest published version. The role is Owner, Admin, Member or Viewer,
and it is blank for public work you reached without one. Something you can no
longer open, something deleted and an archived experiment all drop out of the
list. The platform keeps your last 50 visits across every type.

Once you have opened more than one type, chips under the heading narrow the
list: **All**, then one for each type you have opened. Picking a type shows its
five newest and a link to that type's own list, such as **See all protocols**.
Your browser remembers the choice, and goes back to **All** when nothing of that
type is left to show.

Before your first visit, the section shows **Your experiments** instead: work
you created, work shared with you and work in your organizations, newest update
first, with the date of each update. **See all** opens the experiments list.

### Get started

If you have no experiments yet, **Get started** sits at the top of Home with
three places to begin. **Create an experiment** opens the new experiment form,
**Join an organization** opens the organizations list, where you can ask to join
a public organization, and **Browse public research** opens the experiments list
filtered to public experiments, with the search box ready to type in.

### Public research and blog posts

**Public research** shows recently updated public experiments, newest update
first, as many as fill its rows evenly. An update means someone changed the
experiment itself: its details, locations, flow or workbook. Making it public or
moving it to another organization also counts. New measurements do not.
Embargoed experiments stay private until the embargo ends, so they never appear
here. When no public experiment has been updated for 30 days, the section says
so and shows the date of the latest update. **See all** opens the experiments
list filtered to public experiments.

**Recent blog posts** shows the three newest posts from the openJII blog in one
row, and **See all** opens the blog.

### Beside the main column

**Notifications** lists up to five unread notifications, and its heading counts
every unread one, as the bell does. Opening one marks it read and takes you to
what it is about. When nothing is unread it says you are all caught up, and
**See all** opens the Notifications page.

**Your last 30 days** shows one figure at a time and moves to the next every
few seconds. It starts with the measurements your experiments recorded,
compared with the 30 days before and drawn as a line. Then come your daily
average with the busiest day and how many days recorded anything, the
experiments that were active with the contributors and devices behind them, and
what the whole community recorded over the same period. The dots under it pick
a figure, and the rotation holds while your pointer or focus is on it. The pause
button stops it, and it starts paused if your system asks for less motion.

**What's new** lists the two newest release notes, each opening its release
page in a new tab, and **See all** opens the What's new panel. Under it, **Report a bug** opens
[Getting help](/guide/reference/getting-help) and **request a feature** opens
the ideas board on GitHub.

## Platform activity

Several pages carry a row of activity cards covering the last 30 days. The
experiment, protocol, macro and workbook lists carry one for that kind,
including the busiest one, and each row in the table draws its own activity
strip. An experiment's overview page has the same row under its description,
and Home sums up your own in **Your last 30 days**.

Contributors and devices are counted separately because measurements arrive by
two routes: the mobile app records who took a measurement, while a logger
publishes on its own and names nobody. An experiment fed only by loggers shows
the devices that reported rather than a contributor count.

The figures come from the platform metrics pipeline, which refreshes them every
hour, and the platform caches them for up to another hour. Anything with
nothing to report is left out rather than shown as a zero.

## Account settings

Open your profile menu at the bottom of the sidebar and select **Account** to
reach your account pages. They have five tabs:

- **General** for your profile details.
- **Security** for passkeys and sign-in methods.
- **API keys** for keys that let scripts call the openJII API on your behalf.
- **Invitations** for invitations to join an organization that are waiting for
  you, each with **Accept invitation** and **Decline**. See
  [Find and join an organization](/guide/organizations/finding-and-joining#if-you-were-invited-instead).
- **Notifications** for the email switches described
  [above](#which-notifications-also-reach-you-by-email).

See [Account security and API keys](/guide/get-started/account-security-api-keys)
for the Security and API keys tabs.
