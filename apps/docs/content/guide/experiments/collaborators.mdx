Experiments are meant to be shared. Everyone you share an experiment with directly
appears in one list, and each row carries one setting: how much they can do.

The list is that set of direct shares — users, teams and organizations someone added
on purpose. It is not a full audit of who can open the experiment: members of the
organization that owns it reach it through their organization role, and a public
experiment is readable by anyone.

Those people are not simply missing. Owners of the owning organization are named
individually; its admins and members appear as counted summary rows; anyone who also
holds a share is broken out into their own row and subtracted from those counts; and
public readers get no row at all. A row may also carry a **Redundant grant** badge,
meaning the share adds nothing to access the person already has.

<Callout type="info">
  [Who can access your work?](/guide/access) explains the whole model in one page — the three routes
  in, why the strongest wins, and how to read a row. Start there if someone has access you cannot
  account for.
</Callout>

## The two access levels

- **Can view** — open the experiment, read its data and visualizations, **and
  contribute**: take measurements with the mobile app, and add annotations and
  comments. This is usually the person in the field.
- **Can edit** — everything above, plus the experiment's settings, publishing,
  archiving, and managing who else has access.

### What each level can do

| Action                                                   | Can view | Can edit |
| :------------------------------------------------------- | :------: | :------: |
| See the experiment, its data and visualizations          |    ✓     |    ✓     |
| Take measurements with the mobile app                    |    ✓     |    ✓     |
| Add annotations, comments and flags                      |    ✓     |    ✓     |
| Upload measurement data on the web                       |    ✓     |    ✓     |
| Add or edit experiment metadata                          |    –     |    ✓     |
| Edit experiment settings (name, description, visibility) |    –     |    ✓     |
| Create and edit visualizations                           |    –     |    ✓     |
| Attach or change the workbook, and edit locations        |    –     |    ✓     |
| Add or remove collaborators and change their level       |    –     |    ✓     |
| Archive the experiment                                   |    –     |    ✓     |

<Callout type="info">
  **Being added is what lets someone contribute.** Making an experiment public lets anyone *read* it
  — it never lets a stranger add measurements or annotations. Only people who hold an access level
  on the experiment can contribute, which is why the lowest level is still called "Can view" rather
  than "read-only".
</Callout>

## Invite a collaborator

Open your experiment and go to the **Collaborators** tab, then select **Invite**.
One search field covers every way of adding someone:

- **A user** — search by name or email and select them. They get access
  immediately at the level you choose.
- **An organization** — switch **Share with** to _Organization_ and pick one of
  your own. Everyone in that organization gets the level you choose.
- **Someone by email** — type an email address that is not yet on openJII and
  choose **Send invite by email**. They receive an email, and they join the
  experiment at the chosen level once they create an account.

Choose the access level in the same dialog. New collaborators default to **Can
view**, which is the level that lets them contribute.

The Collaborators tab groups people into three sub-tabs:

- **Collaborators** — the users and organizations the experiment is shared with
  directly, each with their level.
- **Invited** — pending email invitations, each showing the level it will confer.
  Revoke one before it is accepted and re-invite if you need to change that level.
- **Requests** — pending requests to join (see below).

The filter above the tabs narrows all three lists at once.

## Requests to join a public experiment

Anyone viewing a **public** experiment they are not part of can select **Request
to join**, with an optional message. People who can manage the experiment see
these under the **Requests** sub-tab and can **Approve** or **Reject** them.
Approved people join with **Can view**, so they can start contributing.

## Join codes

Approving thirty people one at a time does not work in a workshop. A **join code**
is the way round it: an eight-character code, shown as `XXXX-XXXX` with a QR
beside it, that anyone signed in to the Android app can enter to join the
experiment straight away. No request, no approval. They join with **Can view**,
the level that lets them measure.

The card is on the **Collaborators** tab, above the collaborator list. It only
appears on a **public** experiment that is not archived, and only for people who
can manage access. A private experiment has no card at all; publish it from the
**Overview** tab first, and note that publishing cannot be undone.

![The join code card with its code, QR and redemption counter](/img/guide/web/join-code-card.webp)

<Callout type="warn">
  Participants can only measure if the attached workbook is public too. The code grants **Can view**
  on the *experiment*, which says nothing about the workbook behind it. A participant who joins a
  public experiment whose workbook is private gets as far as the measurement screen and is told the
  workbook is not shared with them. Check the workbook's visibility before the workshop.
</Callout>

### Creating, sharing and ending one

Pick how long the code should last, then select **Create join code**. The choices
are 1 day, 7 days, 30 days, and no expiry; 7 days is the default. The card then
shows the code in large type, its QR, the expiry date and how many people have
used it. That counter updates on its own while the card is open, so you can leave
it projected during a session and watch the number climb.

**Copy code** copies the code itself, for reading out or writing on a whiteboard.
**Copy link** copies the address the QR encodes, which is a page that hands the
visitor to the app. Both stay valid until the code expires or you end it.

An experiment has **one** active code at a time.

- **Regenerate** issues a new code and stops the old one.
- **Revoke** stops the current code without issuing another.

Either way, anyone who already joined keeps their access. Ending a code only
closes the door behind them, which is what makes regenerating between two
workshops safe.

An expired code is not deleted. The card shows a banner naming it and the date it
ran out, with the expiry picker and **Create a new code** beside it.

### What participants see

The QR encodes a public page showing the code as text, a link that opens the app,
and a link to the Play Store for anyone who does not have it yet. The code itself
is the fallback: it can always be typed in by hand. Their side of this is in
[Join an experiment from the app](/guide/measuring/joining-an-experiment).

## Changing a level, removing someone, leaving

From the **Collaborators** sub-tab, change anyone's level with the dropdown on
their row, or remove them with the bin button next to it. Your own row is marked
**(you)** and its button is **Leave**, which gives up your own access.

Some rows carry an **Outside Collaborator** label. That is information, not a
restriction: it marks a person or organization from outside the organization that
owns the experiment, so you can see at a glance who is external. What they can do
still comes from their access level.

<Callout type="info">
  What has to hold is that the experiment stays administrable — not that it keeps a **Can edit**
  collaborator. While the organization that owns it still has an owner, that owner is in full
  control, so you can remove or lower anyone here, including the last **Can edit**. The refusal
  appears only once that organization has no owner left and this row is the last *person* who could
  still administer the experiment — a shared organization does not count. The reason is shown when
  it does.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
  Removing someone takes away *this* access. They may still be able to reach the experiment another
  way — through the organization that owns it, through an organization or team they belong to that
  was also given access, or because the experiment is public. The confirmation dialog says so; check
  those routes too if the intent is to remove access entirely.
</Callout>

## Who can see the list

The collaborator list, the pending invitations and the access levels are only
visible to people who can manage access — that is, holders of **Can edit** and
admins of the owning organization. Everyone else sees the experiment's
contributor credit (names and avatars) and nothing about who holds which level.

If you hold **Can view**, the Collaborators tab shows you a **Your access** card
instead of the list, with a **Leave** button for giving up your own access.

For access to macros, protocols, workbooks and devices, see
[Sharing & Collaboration](/guide/sharing) — those resources use the same two
levels on their own **Collaborators** tab, except that a device is never
publishable.
