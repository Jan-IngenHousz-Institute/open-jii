A **team** is a named subgroup inside one organization — a field crew, an analysis
group, a cohort. Its only purpose is to be shared with: give a team access to an
experiment and everyone in the team gets it.

Teams are flat. There is no nesting, and a team belongs to exactly one organization.

## Create a team and add people

Open the organization, go to **Teams**, and select **Create team**. Give it a name, then
add members from the organization.

You can only add people who already belong to the organization. If someone is missing,
[invite them to the organization](/guide/organizations/people-and-roles) first.

## Sharing with a team

On any resource's **Collaborators** tab, share with a team the same way you would share
with a person, and choose **Can view** or **Can edit**. Everyone in the team gets that
level, and the list shows one row for the team — not one per member.

Adding someone to the team later gives them that access too, with nothing to update on
the resource.

The team's own page is where you can see everything it reaches:

![A team's page showing its members above a What this team can reach list, naming the experiments and the device shared with the team, each at Can view](/img/guide/web/organization-team.webp)

That list is the team's own shares, not what its people can reach by other routes — which
is why it is exactly what deleting the team would withdraw.

<Callout type="info">
  A team share is one of the three ways someone reaches a resource. It stacks with their
  organization role rather than replacing it — the strongest applies. See [Who can access your
  work?](/guide/access).
</Callout>

## What deleting a team does

Deleting a team **withdraws every share that team held**. Anyone whose only access to a
resource came through that team loses it immediately.

People are not removed from the organization, and access they hold another way — their
organization role, or a share in their own name — is untouched.

<Callout type="warn">
  Removing someone from a team is not the same as removing their access. If they can still open the
  resource afterwards, they have another route in: their role in the organization that owns it, a
  share of their own, or public visibility.
</Callout>
