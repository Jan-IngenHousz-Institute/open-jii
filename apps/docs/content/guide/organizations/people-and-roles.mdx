Open an organization and go to **Members**. Everything about who belongs and what they
can do happens on this one screen, across three sub-tabs:

- **Members** — everyone who belongs now, with their role.
- **Invited** — invitations sent and not yet accepted.
- **Requests** — people asking to join, if the organization is public.

![The Members screen of an organization, with the Members, Invited and Requests segments each showing a count, above a roster listing every member with their role and team badges](/img/guide/web/organization-members.webp)

The three segments only appear for Owners and Admins. A Member sees the roster alone,
because the other two are decisions they cannot take.

## What each role can do

|                                                            | Owner | Admin | Member |
| :--------------------------------------------------------- | :---: | :---: | :----: |
| Read everything the organization owns                      |   ✓   |   ✓   |   ✓    |
| Contribute measurements and annotations to its experiments |   ✓   |   ✓   |   ✓    |
| Edit, share and delete what it owns                        |   ✓   |   ✓   |   –    |
| Invite, remove, and change roles                           |   ✓   |   ✓   |   –    |
| Make someone else an Owner                                 |   ✓   |   –   |   –    |
| Change the organization's name, slug and profile           |   ✓   |   –   |   –    |
| Delete the organization                                    |   ✓   |   –   |   –    |

<Callout type="info">
  Admins and Owners are equally powerful over the organization's **work**. The difference is
  authority over the **organization itself**.
</Callout>

## Invite someone

Select **Invite**, search for someone by name or type an email address, and choose the
role they will get. Whether or not they already have an openJII account, the result is
the same: an invitation. **Nobody joins an organization they did not ask to join** — a
membership carries read access to everything the organization owns, so it begins when
the person accepts, not when you decide.

So the **Members** tab does not move when you invite somebody. They appear on
**Invited** until they accept, and you can withdraw the invitation from there at any
time.

**No role is ever accepted for them.** Whatever the role, and whether or not they
already had an account, the invitation waits on their own **Account → Invitations** tab
until they accept it there, with the role on screen. Signing in — including signing up
for the first time from the invitation email, and including ORCID and GitHub — does not
accept anything.

So the flow for someone brand new is: they follow the link in the email, sign up, and
the invitation is still there waiting on that tab, with **Accept** on it.

<Callout type="warn">
  Invitations are matched on the **email address**, not the person. If you invite an address they
  never sign in with, nothing will ever happen. Check the address on the **Invited** tab before
  assuming the invitation is broken, and re-send to the right one.
</Callout>

Invitations expire. An expired one has to be sent again.

## Approve or reject a request

If your organization is public, people can ask to join from the directory. Pending
requests appear on the **Requests** tab with any message they wrote. **Approve** adds
them as a Member; **Reject** declines without adding them.

This is the one path that puts somebody on the roster straight away, and it is the same
rule seen from the other side: they asked, so there is nothing left for them to accept.

## Change a role, or remove someone

On the **Members** tab, change a role with the dropdown on the person's row, or remove
them with the button beside it.

<Callout type="warn">
  Removing someone from the organization does not remove access they hold another way. If they also
  hold a share on a resource — in their own name, or through a team or another organization — that
  share survives and they keep it. They will simply appear as an **Outside Collaborator** from then
  on. See [Who can access your work?](/guide/access).
</Callout>

## Leave an organization

Your own row is marked **(you)** and its action is **Leave**.

You cannot leave if you are the only Owner. Make someone else an Owner first, or
[delete the organization](/guide/organizations/deleting) if it has served its purpose.
The same rule blocks deleting your openJII account while you are an organization's last
Owner.
