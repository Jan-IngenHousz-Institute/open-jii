FAIR means **Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable**. Open data and
FAIR data overlap, but they are not the same: data can be private under a valid
access policy and still follow FAIR practices, while a public file with no
metadata may be difficult to reuse.

openJII captures the core descriptive metadata for you and shows how complete
each experiment is. It does not certify FAIR compliance.

## What the platform captures

### Study details

Every experiment can carry six study descriptors, entered during creation or
any time from the experiment page:

| Field               | Why it matters                                                      |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start and end date  | when the study ran; required by metadata standards and repositories |
| Organism            | what was studied; the most common search facet for reuse            |
| Experimental design | how plots, treatments, and replicates were arranged                 |
| Growth facility     | field, greenhouse, or growth chamber context                        |
| Data license        | whether and how others may reuse the data                           |

These fields are optional and never block your work. They map onto the
[MIAPPE](https://www.miappe.org/) checklist, the community's minimum
information standard for plant phenotyping experiments, so filling them in
prepares the experiment for standards-compliant sharing.

### Standards readiness

The experiment page shows how many study descriptors are filled and whether a
license is chosen, so completeness is visible at a glance rather than a
surprise at sharing time.

## The license choice

openJII offers two data licenses, the two accepted by research funders and
repositories:

- **CC0 1.0**: no conditions; maximum reusability. Scholarly citation is still
  expected by community norms.
- **CC BY 4.0**: reuse requires attribution as a legal condition.

Choosing a license is optional while you work. An experiment without one is
viewable according to its visibility settings, but its data is not licensed
for reuse outside the platform.

## A practical checklist

- Give the experiment and datasets descriptive names, and fill the study
  details above.
- Choose public visibility or document the embargo and access conditions.
- Export in a documented standard format such as CSV, JSON, Parquet, or Excel.
- Use stable column names, explicit units, and ISO dates; explain nested
  values, raw traces, flags, and missing values.
- Record the protocol, workbook version, hardware, and macros used; include
  quality annotations and known limitations.
- Where possible, publish analysis code and an environment lockfile or
  container recipe so another researcher can reproduce the transformation.
