When you create an experiment, macro, protocol, workbook or device, the form asks which
workspace should own it. It is a small dropdown with a large consequence, so it is worth
understanding once rather than discovering later.

## The two answers

**Your personal workspace** is the default. The resource is yours: nobody else can open
it unless you share it with them individually, and nobody can be added to your personal
workspace to gain access in bulk.

**A shared organization** means the organization owns it, and its people can reach it
immediately:

- **Owners and Admins** can edit, share and delete it — straight away, without you
  sharing anything.
- **Members** can read it. On an experiment they can also contribute measurements and
  annotations.

<Callout type="warn">
  Picking an organization grants access to everyone already in it, at the moment you create. That is
  the point of a shared workspace, but it is not reversible by "unsharing" — there is no share to
  remove. To narrow it afterwards you have to [move the resource](/guide/sharing/moving-resources)
  somewhere else.
</Callout>

## Which to pick

Choose the **organization** when the work belongs to the group: the lab paid for it, the
group will keep using it, and it should outlast your involvement.

Choose your **personal workspace** when the work is exploratory, unfinished, or genuinely
yours — then move it into the organization when it is ready. Moving is a supported step,
not a workaround.

<Callout type="info">
  Only shared organizations you belong to appear in the picker. Your personal workspace is always
  there. Other people's organizations never are.
</Callout>

## Changing your mind

The owning workspace is shown on every resource's detail page as **Owning organization**,
and can be changed later — except for devices, which cannot be moved at all. See
[Moving a resource](/guide/sharing/moving-resources).

That makes the device picker the one choice worth pausing over: for a device, the
workspace you pick at registration is the one it keeps for its whole life.
