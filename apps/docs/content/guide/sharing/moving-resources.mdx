Work created in your personal workspace can be moved into a lab, and work owned by one
organization can be moved to another. This is the fix for "I built this on my own and
now the group needs it".

<Callout type="info">
  This is not [Project transfers](/guide/sharing/project-transfers), which is about migrating a
  PhotosynQ project into openJII. This page is about work that is already in openJII changing which
  workspace owns it.
</Callout>

## Move a resource

Open the resource and find **Owning organization** on its detail page. Select
**Transfer**, choose the destination, and confirm.

![The Transfer dialog with its destination list open, showing another organization above a separately grouped Personal workspace option captioned Only you, no organization members come with it](/img/guide/web/resource-transfer.png)

Your personal workspace is listed apart from the organizations on purpose: it is the one
destination that takes the resource away from everybody but you.

## What you need to be allowed to do it

Both sides are checked:

- **On the side losing it** — you need full control of the organization that owns it
  now: Owner or Admin. Being a Member is not enough.
- **On the side receiving it** — you need to be able to create there: a member of the
  destination organization, or it is your own personal workspace.

<Callout type="info">
  There is one narrow exception on the losing side. If the organization has no living Owner or Admin
  left, someone who holds Can edit on the resource itself may move it out — otherwise the work would
  be stranded in an organization nobody can act on.
</Callout>

## Devices cannot be moved

Experiments, macros, protocols and workbooks can all be transferred. **Devices cannot** —
there is no transfer route for them at all. A device stays with the organization that
registered it until the device is deleted.

This matters when you are trying to empty an organization before
[deleting it](/guide/organizations/deleting).

## What changes, and what doesn't

|                                    | After the move                                                                                   |
| :--------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The destination's roles            | Start applying immediately — its Owners and Admins can edit, its Members can read and contribute |
| The old organization's roles       | Stop applying. People who only had access through it lose it                                     |
| Shares held by a **person**        | Survive                                                                                          |
| Shares held by an **organization** | Survive                                                                                          |
| Shares held by a **team**          | **Removed.** Every team share on the resource is withdrawn by the move                           |
| Visibility and embargo             | Unchanged. Moving does not publish or unpublish anything                                         |
| The data itself                    | Unchanged — measurements, annotations and versions all move with it                              |

<Callout type="warn">
  The team share removal is the one that surprises people. If a field crew reached the experiment
  through a team, they lose it the moment it moves — re-share with a team in the destination
  organization afterwards.
</Callout>

## After moving

Check the Collaborators tab. The list is rebuilt around the new owner, so the summary
rows now count the destination's admins and members, and anyone who kept a share is
shown against the new organization. See
[Who can access your work?](/guide/access) for how to read it.
